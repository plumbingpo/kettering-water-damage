How Akron Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in Akron homes started with something small that gave warning first. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Begin at the hoses. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. A stain that spreads or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, and Akron homeowners are welcome to ask us about it.

What Usually Goes Wrong in Akron Homes

Most water damage calls we take in Akron trace back to a short list of causes. If you can spot these early, you have a good chance of stopping the damage before it spreads.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: Washing machines, dishwashers and refrigerator ice makers all depend on small hoses that wear out. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. The crew from Kettering Restoration can dry the attic and ceiling, but the roof itself needs a repair to stop the leak.

Basement: In homes that have a basement, groundwater pushes through foundation cracks and floor joints when the soil is saturated. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. A lot of the mold we find in Akron comes from a past leak that looked dry on the surface only. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.

Drying Out an Akron Property: What to Expect

Homeowners in Akron often want to know what living with drying equipment is actually like. On day one, the crew extracts the water, removes anything that cannot be dried, and sets air movers and dehumidifiers in the affected rooms. Expect noise on the level of a few big fans, plus noticeable warmth in the room. It has to run day and night, so please do not switch it off, move it, or open windows unless we ask you to.

Each day after that, a technician visits to take moisture readings in the same spots and record them. As areas dry, we move machines to the places that are lagging and take away the ones no longer needed. You will see the numbers and hear what they mean.

A typical job takes 3 to 5 days of drying. It can run longer when water sat for a while before it was found, when it reached insulation or hardwood, or when several floors are involved. When every reading matches the dry standard, Kettering Restoration removes the equipment and gives you the drying record for your Akron property.
